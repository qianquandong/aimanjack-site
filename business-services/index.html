How we start

What the first two weeks look like

The first week is mostly listening. We have a short call to find the one customer journey worth fixing, and then I document the trigger, the current steps, who owns each handoff, and where follow-up actually breaks. Nothing gets bought and nothing changes yet — the goal is a clear picture of the single bottleneck and an honest read on whether AI even helps. If a checklist or a setting you already own would solve it, that is where the audit ends, and you have not spent anything on a build you did not need.

The second week is a small, working build: the website, or the one follow-up you can test, kept as narrow as possible. You see it before anything goes live, we adjust the wording and the steps together, and only then does it start handling real customers. After launch the changes stay small and continuous — a new service to list, a seasonal message, a workflow that has earned its place — which is what the monthly plan covers. You are never waiting on an agency queue to change a line on your own site.

Questions about AI consulting and automation in Dallas

What does an AI consultant do for a small business?

I map one customer or operational workflow, identify where work breaks down, recommend what should and should not be automated, and can build the smallest useful solution.

Are you an AI automation agency?

I provide focused AI automation services without a large-agency engagement. You work directly with me on the audit, build, launch, and maintenance.

What should a small business automate first?

Start with a frequent, specific task that has a clear trigger and an output a person can verify. Missed-call follow-up, review requests, and customer win-back messages are common starting points.

Do I need to replace my current software?

Usually not. The first goal is to improve the tools and customer journey you already have. I recommend new software only when the current setup cannot support the needed workflow.
