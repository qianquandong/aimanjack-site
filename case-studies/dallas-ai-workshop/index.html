What happened: Jack led a 2.5-hour community AI workshop in Dallas showing how Claude Code, OpenClaw, and multiple specialized AI agents can work together as a practical team. The examples connected Multi-Agent systems to e-commerce, teaching, customer service, and software-development workflows.

Why the workshop focused on Multi-Agent workflows

Most professionals first meet AI as a single chat window. That is useful, but many real jobs involve several different responsibilities: gathering information, organizing it, drafting an output, checking the result, and handing the work to another person.

The workshop introduced a different model. Instead of asking one assistant to do everything, participants explored how several agents can take distinct roles and pass work between them. The goal was not complexity for its own sake. It was to make each responsibility clearer and easier to review.

Four workflow examples from the session

E-commerce

Product selection, listing copy, publishing, customer service, and performance review.

Education

Lesson planning, presentation creation, practice generation, and student feedback.

Customer service

Issue classification, response drafting, knowledge-base maintenance, and ticket summaries.

Software development

Requirement breakdown, implementation, testing, review, and documentation.

Who the event was designed for

The published event description welcomed engineers, entrepreneurs, e-commerce professionals, educators, content creators, product managers, students, and anyone interested in using AI to improve everyday work. That mix matters: Multi-Agent workflows are not only a programming topic. The same role-based thinking can help any team separate work into understandable steps.
