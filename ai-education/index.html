The outcome

AI training should change how work gets done

AI Man Jack is a free community AI education initiative in Dallas–Fort Worth. The goal is not to memorize AI vocabulary. It is to identify a real task, choose the right tool, build a repeatable workflow, and know where human judgment still belongs.

Most people do not need another broad tour of every new AI product. They need help with the work already sitting in front of them: an inbox that will not stay organized, a long document that needs a reliable summary, a spreadsheet that needs cleanup, or a research task that takes too long. Those are the starting points for each session.

Choose the right tool

Know when a chatbot is enough, when an agent can take useful action, and when AI adds more risk than value.

Build a repeatable workflow

Turn a one-off demo into clear steps you can run again, check, and improve after the workshop.

Use practical guardrails

Protect customer and company information, verify important outputs, and keep a human in charge of decisions.

Sample curriculum

What your team can learn

The exact agenda depends on the participants and their approved tools. A practical session usually moves through four layers:

  1. Find the right task. Separate repetitive information work from decisions that still need human judgment. A good first workflow is frequent, specific, and easy to verify.
  2. Give AI useful context. Learn a prompt structure that explains the goal, source material, constraints, output format, and quality check without relying on “magic words.”
  3. Move from chat to workflow. Connect the steps around a task such as summarizing a document, preparing for a meeting, drafting a response, cleaning a spreadsheet, or turning email into an action list.
  4. Add checks and guardrails. Review sources, protect sensitive data, define where approval is required, and document the workflow so another person can run it.

Frequently asked questions

Questions about AI training in Dallas

Do participants need coding experience?

No. The training is designed for people who can use a browser, copy and paste, and describe the work they do. Coding is not required.

Are the community workshops free?

Yes. The community education events and online resources are currently offered at no charge. Capacity, registration, and venue rules may vary by event.

Is this an AI course or certification program?

It is a practical community learning series, not a degree, bootcamp, or certification program. You learn through beginner-friendly classes, hands-on workshops, and build events, but no formal certificate is issued.

Do you offer in-person AI training in Dallas?

Yes. I offer hands-on, in-person training for professionals, small teams, associations, and community groups in the Dallas–Fort Worth area.

What AI tools do you teach?

The tools depend on the work. The training explains when a chatbot is enough, when an agent can help, and when AI is the wrong tool. The goal is a useful workflow, not loyalty to one product.

How do you handle company data and privacy?

Every session includes practical guardrails: what not to paste into an AI tool, how to remove sensitive information, how to follow your company's approved-tool policy, and where human review is required. You can also use the AI data-safety checklist for teams before a workshop.

How can I attend or invite a community session?

Use the Events page to follow the published Luma listings. Community groups can also email Jack with the audience, preferred language, location, and one practical topic they want to explore.
