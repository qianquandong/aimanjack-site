Your work is on Instagram. Your next client is on Google.

Most salons run on Instagram and word of mouth — and that works, until you want growth you can count on. When someone new to the neighborhood searches "hair salon near me" or "nail salon Allen TX," Instagram doesn't show up. A website with a tuned Google Business Profile does.

A salon website has one job: get the visitor to book. Every build below puts the booking link front and center, loads fast on a phone, and connects to a Google Business Profile so the salon shows up on the map when nearby clients search.

The client this is built for is specific: a woman who just moved to Plano and needs someone who can do her color, standing in her kitchen on a Sunday night with her phone. She searches, taps the first three salons, and books whichever one shows her real work, a real price, and a booking button she can press right then. She is not going to fill out a contact form and wait until Tuesday. Every choice on these builds is aimed at that ninety seconds.

What a salon site needs (and what it doesn't)

  • Booking first: your booking link — Square Appointments, StyleSeat, Booksy, Vagaro, whatever you already use — one tap from every page. Most salon sites bury it behind a contact form, and a client who has to type her name and wait for a callback books somewhere else instead. One tap means she books before she closes the tab. The site feeds your system, it doesn't replace it.
  • Services and prices: written as text on the page, not locked in a PDF or a photo of your menu. A client comparing three salons at eleven at night wants the balayage price without calling — and text is also the only version Google and ChatGPT can read, so a price list as an image makes you invisible to both.
  • Your actual work: a gallery pulled from the photos you already take, not stock images of models. Stock photos appear on a thousand other salon sites, so a visitor can't tell what you'd actually do to her hair — your own work is the only thing that answers that.
  • Found on the map: Google Business Profile filled out, verified, and tuned. Google ranks the listings it can confirm the most about, so the primary category, the service list, the hours, and a phone number matching your site are what move you into the local three-pack — the block of shops that shows above every other result on a phone.
  • Bilingual if your chairs are: the CC Hair build below serves both English and Chinese-speaking clients.

I already use Booksy, StyleSeat, or Square. Do I still need a website?

Yes, and you keep the booking app. Those apps work once someone has already decided on you. They don't get you found when a new neighbor searches "hair salon near me." The website is what Google ranks and what a client checks before booking; your booking app is what closes it. The site feeds your system, it never replaces it.
