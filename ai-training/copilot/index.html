Questions

Microsoft Copilot training, answered

Do we need Microsoft 365 Copilot licenses for the training?

For the in-app features (Copilot in Word, Excel, Outlook, Teams and PowerPoint), yes: each attendee needs the license on the account they use in the session. If your team only has Copilot Chat, we train on that and show what the license would add, so you can decide where it is worth paying for.

Is our company data safe with Copilot?

Copilot respects the access your company already set, so it does not open anything a person could not open before. The real risk is over-shared files, so the first part of the session covers which sites and folders are in scope and what never goes into a prompt. We review Microsoft’s published data terms with your IT lead before the session.

Does our IT team need to be involved?

Briefly. We ask your IT lead to confirm licenses, which apps are enabled, whether meeting transcription is on, and whether agents are allowed. A 15-minute call is usually enough.

Can you teach Copilot in Excel to non-technical staff?

Yes, and it is usually the most useful hour. People learn to ask for summaries, formulas and charts in plain English, and then to check the result against the source before it is used.

What about Copilot agents and Copilot Studio?

We cover where an agent helps with a task that repeats, what it can read, and who should approve it. Building production agents in Copilot Studio is a separate project; the training gets your team to the point of knowing which one is worth building.

We use Google Workspace or ChatGPT too. Is that a problem?

No. The method is the same across tools, and many teams use two. See ChatGPT training or Claude training, or ask for a session that covers both.

What does each person leave with?

At least one Copilot workflow on a task from their own week, written down so a colleague could repeat it, and a short checklist of what to verify before the output is used.

Do people get a certificate?

No. The outcome is a working workflow on a task each person already does, plus a checklist for checking the output. Two weeks after a program ends, Jack checks which workflows were kept, changed or stopped.
