Where a person stays in charge

  • Anything that identifies a client, a case or a minor stays out of AI tools unless your organization has approved the tool and the process.
  • Grant submissions, financial figures and outcome data are checked against your records by a named person before they go out.
  • Letters to donors are approved by a person before sending; no tool sends on its own.
  • Decisions about services, eligibility and staff are made by people, not by a model.

We agree on these rules with your executive director or program lead before the session and write them on one page the staff keeps.

Questions

AI training for nonprofits, answered

Our staff are stretched and not technical. Is this realistic?

Yes. No coding is involved, and each person works on writing they already do, so each session aims to produce a reusable workflow. You can start with a 90-minute workshop on one or two tasks.

Can AI help with grant writing?

With first drafts and restructuring, built from your own approved language and program descriptions. It can also produce numbers or outcomes that sound right and are not, so every figure is checked against your records and a person owns the final submission. Check each funder’s own rules on AI use.

Is it safe to use AI with client information?

Treat it as off limits by default. Client names, case notes and health, immigration or minors’ records stay out unless your organization has approved a specific tool and process for them. Most of the useful work, such as grant drafts, donor letters and board updates, does not need client data at all.

Which AI tools do you teach?

Tools your organization has approved, with the accounts and data terms confirmed, such as Microsoft Copilot, ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini. The workflows carry across tools.

Can our executive director or board members join?

Yes. Leaders and board members can join a session on governance: which tools are approved, the data boundary and who signs off. See also leadership team training.

What does each person leave with?

At least one working workflow on their own material, such as a grant section, a donor letter or board minutes, written down so a colleague could repeat it, plus the one-page data boundary.

How much does AI training for nonprofits cost?

It depends on how many people attend, the format and how much preparation your workflows need, so each team gets its own quote. Use the short form at the bottom of this page: your name, email, headcount and, if you have one, a budget range. Jack replies by email with a recommended format and a written price. Nothing is agreed until you confirm it in writing.

Do you come onsite in Dallas–Fort Worth?

Yes. Onsite sessions run at your office anywhere in the metroplex: Dallas, Fort Worth, Plano, Richardson, Frisco, McKinney, Irving, Arlington and the cities between. Elsewhere in Texas is available on request, with travel quoted separately. Live online sessions work for teams anywhere in the U.S.

How many people can attend?

It is agreed in the quote. Every format is hands-on, so Jack needs to see each person’s work; larger groups are split into two sessions or run as a full day with department blocks.

Is it taught in English or Chinese?

Either. Sessions run in whichever language the room works in, and the materials come in both.

Do people get a certificate?

No. The outcome is a working workflow on a task each person already does, plus a checklist for checking the output. Two weeks after a program ends, Jack checks which workflows were kept, changed or stopped.
