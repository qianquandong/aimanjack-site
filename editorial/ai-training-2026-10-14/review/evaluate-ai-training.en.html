How to evaluate AI training after your team tries the work

Record the starting task, trial the workflow and review what happened. A fluent demonstration and a completed class are not yet evidence of business value.

Proposed title: Evaluate AI Training on Real Team Tasks | AI Man Jack
Description: Evaluate AI training with a task baseline, usage record, accuracy checklist and review-effort log. Decide which workflows to continue, change or stop.

  • Evaluate AI training by tracking actual workflow use, output accuracy and the effort required to review and correct the work.
  • Record the current task before the trial so any later comparison includes the same scope and the human work that remains.
  • Continue, change or stop a workflow based on a documented review; no universal productivity target fits every business task.
  • AI Man Jack’s four-session program includes a review two weeks after the final session; extra support after standalone training is separately scoped.

Choose one task before choosing a success metric

To evaluate AI training, begin with a task your team can describe and check. For example, a meeting-notes exercise has an input, a draft action list and a person who can confirm the owner and due date. “Get better at AI” does not define an observable result.

Attendance, satisfaction and a successful classroom demo answer useful but limited questions. They do not establish whether people used the workflow later, whether its output was accurate or whether checking it took more work than the original method. Keep those questions separate in the review.

The framework below is a proposed measurement method. It contains no customer result, claimed saving or typical improvement rate. Set the review period and decision criteria to match the task before the trial begins.

Write a baseline for the current way of working

Use a recent suitable example or observe a normal instance of the task. Record the source material, required output, responsible person and reviewer. Note the time spent preparing, doing, checking and correcting the task, together with any known quality problem. If time was not measured, mark it unknown rather than estimating it later from memory.

Describe the scope precisely. A short meeting and a long meeting with several speakers are different cases; a draft action list is different from a final list checked with the participants. Keep the same definition of finished work when comparing methods, and record material differences between the examples.

Do not place confidential material into a new tool just to build the baseline. Use approved records and follow the company’s data rules. The AI tool approval checklist can help identify tool access and restrictions before a trial.

Trial the workflow with five repeatable steps

  1. Choose suitable work. Pick a recurring task with clear input and a result a knowledgeable person can check.
  2. Provide approved context. State the source material, goal, constraints and output format. Use synthetic, redacted or company-approved material.
  3. Write repeatable instructions. Save the steps and version used, so the team can understand what changed between attempts.
  4. Check the output. Compare it with the source, identify omissions and unsupported claims, and record corrections.
  5. Apply the boundaries. Confirm permitted information, the human approval point and where the draft is allowed to go.

The meeting-notes workflow is one task to inspect. During a trial, use approved tools and keep the existing reviewer responsible. Training should not quietly introduce live-system access or automatic updates.

Keep an actual-use and review-effort log

Copy this worksheet for each workflow. Complete it after an attempt while the details are still available. A skipped attempt is useful evidence too: record whether the reason was lack of access, no suitable task, unclear instructions or something else.

There is no preset passing score in this sheet. The task owner should decide which errors are unacceptable before the trial. A wrong customer name, invented commitment or incorrect owner can matter even when the rest of a draft reads well.

Review accuracy before calculating a saving

Ask the reviewer to check the output against approved sources. For an action list, examine actions, owners, due dates and unresolved questions. An item that was never agreed should not become a fact because the model supplied it confidently. Record whether the error came from missing source information, unclear instructions or an unsupported model addition.

Only compare time when the tasks and output requirements are reasonably comparable and the time was observed. Include preparation, drafting, verification, corrections and approval in both methods. A faster first draft does not establish a net saving if the reviewer then has to reconstruct the source.

Do not extrapolate one exercise into an annual financial return. If you later publish a result, state the task, observation period, number of observed attempts, method, remaining human work and limitations. Get appropriate permission before sharing client material.

Make a continue, change or stop decision

Continue when the responsible reviewer accepts the task under the agreed conditions and the team can explain the steps. Keep human approval and schedule another check if the input or tool changes.

Change when the task appears suitable but the source, instructions or checklist needs improvement. Make one clear revision, save its version and repeat the trial. A changed prompt without a record makes comparison harder.

Stop when the workflow cannot meet the task’s requirements, checking is impractical, required access is unavailable or the material is outside approved data boundaries. Stopping an unsuitable workflow is a valid review outcome.

What AI Man Jack’s follow-up includes

In the four-session program, participants try workflows between meetings and bring back errors, questions and review difficulties. Two weeks after the final session, Jack considers actual use, accuracy and checking effort, then discusses whether each workflow should continue, change or stop.

That review belongs to the four-session program. Extra support after a standalone workshop or onsite day is separately scoped. Training excludes software development and CRM/ERP configuration and integration; connecting live systems or writing back automatically requires separate scope, permissions and testing.

When this evaluation does not work

A comparison does not work when the baseline is missing, the task changes unnoticed or nobody can verify the output. Fix the record or report the result as inconclusive. A two-week window may contain no instance of an infrequent task; that absence cannot establish success or failure, so agree another review time.

For the next step, choose one task and complete the baseline before training. Bring the questions to a workflow call, or use the two-week review template for your own internal review. Keep observations separate from hoped-for results.

Questions and answers

What should we measure after AI training?

Track whether the workflow was used, whether its output met the task’s accuracy and completeness requirements, and how much effort checking and correcting it required. Record the task baseline before comparing results.

Does a faster AI draft prove the training saved time?

No. A comparison needs comparable tasks and observed preparation, drafting, checking, correction and approval time. Faster drafting alone does not establish a net saving.

When does AI Man Jack review the four-session program?

Jack reviews actual use, accuracy and checking effort two weeks after the final session of the four-session program. Extra support after a standalone workshop or onsite day is separately scoped.

What if the team has not used the workflow by the review date?

Record why it was not attempted, such as no suitable task, missing access or unclear instructions. If the task did not occur during the observation period, agree another review date rather than inventing a result.
