AI workflow build

When training is not enough, we build the workflow with you.

Some work needs more than better prompts: it moves between a CRM, spreadsheets, forms and email, or follows rules too strict to leave to a chatbot. Jack scopes it, builds it around the systems you already use, tests it on your real cases, and trains the people who will run it.

Fit

Who a build is for

  • A team with one recurring task that takes hours every week and follows clear rules
  • Work that moves between systems: CRM, spreadsheets, forms, email, Microsoft 365, Google Workspace or Lark
  • A business that wants its rules in a sheet it can edit, not buried in code
  • Teams that already tried a chatbot on the task and got results they could not trust

Not the right fit

  • Replacing an ERP or migrating a whole CRM
  • Projects that need the builder to hold a compliance certification such as HIPAA or SOC 2
  • A product you plan to sell to your own customers
  • Workflows where nobody will review the output
How it works

From one task to a workflow your team runs

  1. 1

    A 20-minute call

    What the task is, who does it now, which systems it touches and what “done” looks like.

  2. 2

    Written scope and quote

    What the workflow will do, what it will not do, which data and accounts it uses, and how we will check it works. Nothing is built before you agree to it.

  3. 3

    Build and test on real cases

    Built against sample or approved data first, then compared with a real week of the work. Problems are fixed before anyone relies on it.

  4. 4

    Run it next to the old way

    A person reviews every output while the workflow runs alongside your current process.

  5. 5

    Hand over and follow up

    Written instructions, a training session for the people who run it, and a check two weeks later on whether it should continue, change or stop.

What you get

Delivered with every build

  • A working workflow that runs in accounts your team controls
  • Business rules kept where your team can change them, such as a spreadsheet
  • A plain-language page explaining what the workflow does and why
  • Automated tests where the build includes code
  • Training for the people who run and review it
  • A two-week follow-up check

Not included

  • AI tool, software or hosting subscriptions
  • Ongoing maintenance or new features, unless scoped separately
  • Changes inside systems your team cannot give us access to

FAQ

Questions about builds

Training or a build: which do we need?

If your team can do the task with an AI tool once they know how, training is enough; start with the training program. If the task needs to run on its own, across systems, or with rules enforced exactly, it is a build. The 20-minute call sorts this out.

Do you work inside our CRM or other systems?

Yes, through access your admin approves. We start with the smallest access that does the job, usually read-only, and agree in writing which data and accounts the workflow touches.

Do you use AI or regular code?

Whichever the task needs. A language model is useful where the input is messy, like free-text notes. Where business rules must hold exactly, the decision is made by deterministic code, as in the scheduling case study.

Who owns what you build?

Where possible it runs in accounts your company owns, and you keep the code, the rules and the documentation.

How long does a build take?

It depends on the task and the systems involved. The written scope gives a timeline before you commit.

Can our team learn to maintain it?

Yes. The handover includes training for the people who run it, and the rules sit where they can change them without a code change.
